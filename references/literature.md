# Literature & grounding

The checker isn't invented — it's a synthesis of the practitioner and research canon on KPI-setting for early-stage (often pre-PMF) startups. Use this when a founder wants the "why," or to cite a source. Honest caveat: the rigorous *research* here (goal-setting, premature scaling) is mostly cautionary; the specific "track this" prescriptions are practitioner doctrine, not peer-reviewed findings.

## Search, not execution
A startup is an organization *searching* for a repeatable model, not executing a known one (Steve Blank). So pre-PMF, metrics exist to drive **learning**, not to report performance. Eric Ries's *validated learning* and the **vanity vs. actionable** metric distinction (cohort-based, cause-and-effect, decision-changing) come from here — as does **innovation accounting** (measure the per-customer engine over successive cohorts).

- Blank, *The Four Steps to the Epiphany*.
- Ries, *The Lean Startup* — vanity vs actionable metrics, innovation accounting.

## Focus and stage
- Croll & Yoskovitz, *Lean Analytics* — the **One Metric That Matters**, and the principle that a good metric is a ratio/rate, comparative, and changes behavior; the OMTM changes by stage.

## Product-market fit is measurable (and dynamic)
- **Sean Ellis 40% test** — % of users "very disappointed" to lose the product; ~40% is the empirical PMF threshold.
- **Rahul Vohra / Superhuman** — turning the Ellis score into a leading-indicator engine.
- **Andreessen / Rachleff** — PMF is when the market *pulls* product out of you; the qualitative counterweight ("you can feel it").
- **Brian Balfour** — retention is the *foundation* of growth (monetization, virality, acquisition payback all derive from it); the PMF signal is a retention curve that **plateaus**, read **by segment**; **PMF is a treadmill** you can lose; and PMF alone isn't enough — you also need the **Four Fits** (market↔product, product↔channel, channel↔model, model↔market).

## The quantitative school (Chamath / Social Capital / Tribe Capital)
PMF leaves a quantitative fingerprint you can read early — the school that runs from Facebook growth → Social Capital → Tribe Capital.
- **Chamath Palihapitiya** — the **magic moment** / core product value (Facebook's "7 friends in 10 days"): work backward from retained users to the one behavior that predicts retention, then drive everyone to it.
- **Jonathan Hsu, "Diligence at Social Capital"** (6-part series) — **growth accounting** (new/retained/resurrected/churned), the **Quick Ratio**, flattening **cohort retention curves**, engagement depth (DAU/MAU, L28, the power-user "smile"), and cohort-based dollar retention. The primitives are **counts**; ratios are derived from them.
- **Tribe Capital** (Sethi, Hsu, Maidenberg, Ellowitz), "Quantifying Product-Market Fit" — the same machinery as an investing engine; hunting **N-of-1** outliers.

## The cautionary research
- **Goal-setting theory** (Locke & Latham) — specific, hard goals drive performance *when task knowledge is high*; under high uncertainty they can backfire, and **learning goals** beat performance goals. This is the theoretical grounding for "measure learning, not output" pre-PMF.
- **Measurement dysfunction** (Austin; Goodhart's / Campbell's law) — once a metric becomes a target it gets gamed. Grounds gate 5.
- **Startup Genome / premature scaling** (Marmer et al.) — scaling spend against growth KPIs *before* validating the model is a leading cause of failure. Grounds the pre-PMF stage-awareness.
